Is the data correct?

How I checked the data

Every number comes from the official GST monthly releases. I also checked that my months add up to the official totals.

Checks I did

  • The 12 months of FY 2025-26 add up to ₹22,27,096 crore. The official full-year total is the same.
  • The 12 months of FY 2024-25 add up to ₹20,55,516 crore. The official total is ₹20,55,515 crore. The ₹1 crore gap is rounding.
  • April to December and April to February running totals also match the official cumulative numbers.
  • For each month, the last-year number comes from the same release as this year's number, so the two are compared fairly.

Good to know

  • Gross GST here is CGST + SGST + IGST. Cess is left out, so both years follow the same rule.
  • News reports in 2025 included cess, so their numbers look bigger. For example, April 2025 was first reported as ₹2,36,716 crore, and without cess it is ₹2,23,265 crore.
  • Official numbers are provisional and can differ by about ₹1 crore because of rounding.
  • Full-year domestic GST is total GST minus imports.

Result

What I found and suggest

What I found

  • GST grew about 8.3% over the full year, to ₹22,27,096 crore.
  • April is the biggest month in both years, at ₹2,23,265 crore in April 2025.
  • Growth was uneven: May grew 17.3%, but November only 0.7%.
  • April to June grew the most (12.4%). October to December grew the least (4.8%).
  • Imports grew 14.1% and domestic GST grew 6.4%. Imports are now 26.7% of the total.

What I would suggest

  1. Plan for April every year, because it is the peak month.
  2. Look into why growth slowed in October to December, after the GST rate cut in late September 2025.
  3. Track imports separately, because they grow faster than domestic GST.
